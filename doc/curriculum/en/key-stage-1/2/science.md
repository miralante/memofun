# Índice
- Working scientifically — Asking simple questions and saying how I might find out
- Working scientifically — Doing a fair test: change one thing, keep the rest the same
- Working scientifically — Measuring with simple tools: a ruler, a balance, a timer
- Working scientifically — Drawing what I see and writing what I notice
- Plants — What plants need to grow and stay healthy: light, water, the right temperature
- Plants — How seeds grow into new plants
- Plants — How plants make their own food (a simple picture, not the chemistry)
- Animals — The basic parts of the human body and what they do
- Animals — The five senses: sight, hearing, touch, taste, smell
- Animals — Keeping healthy: food, water, sleep, exercise
- Animals — Animal offspring: babies grow into adults (caterpillar → butterfly, chick → hen)
- Living things and their habitats — What "living" means: moves, grows, eats, breathes, reproduces
- Living things and their habitats — A habitat is the place where a living thing is at home
- Living things and their habitats — Microhabitats: under a log, in a pond, in long grass
- Living things and their habitats — How animals are suited to where they live
- Everyday materials — Changing the shape of a material: bending, stretching, squashing, twisting
- Everyday materials — Why some materials are better for some jobs: waterproof, absorbent, strong
- Everyday materials — Recycling: paper, plastic, glass, metal can be used again

# Contexto o notas adicionales
Second year of Primary school in England (6-7 years old). Temario
según el English National Curriculum (programmes of study for
Science at Key Stage 1) published by the Department for Education.
Year 2 covers "Uses of everyday materials", "Plants", "Animals,
including humans", and "Living things and their habitats", plus the
"Working scientifically" strands. The first real "habitat" and
"fair test" ideas appear this year — still as concrete everyday
images (a worm under a log, a seed in a pot) rather than as
abstract vocabulary.
