# Índice
- Place knowledge — A region of the UK and a region in North or South America
- Place knowledge — How my UK region compares with a contrasting region overseas
- Human and physical geography — Biomes: tropical rainforest, desert, tundra, savanna, temperate forest, grassland
- Human and physical geography — Where biomes are found on a world map
- Human and physical geography — How plants and animals are suited to their biome
- Human and physical geography — Vegetation belts: what grows where and why
- Human and physical geography — The distribution of natural resources: food, water, energy, minerals
- Human and physical geography — How people use and trade natural resources
- Human and physical geography — The food supply chain: from farm to plate
- Human and physical geography — Sustainable development: looking after resources for the future
- Geographical skills — Reading OS maps at a range of scales
- Geographical skills — Drawing a sketch map with a key, a scale and a north arrow
- Geographical skills — Using digital mapping to explore a place
- Geographical skills — Reading climate graphs: temperature and rainfall through the year
- Fieldwork — Collecting data about human or physical features in the local area

# Contexto o notas adicionales
First year of Upper Key Stage 2 in England (9-10 years old). Temario
según el English National Curriculum (programmes of study for
Geography at Key Stage 2) published by the Department for Education.
Year 5 swaps the European contrast region for an American one and
moves from rivers and water cycles (Year 4) to biomes, natural
resources and the food supply chain. Climate graphs arrive in Year
5 — a key skill for reading what a biome or country is actually
like from its data.
