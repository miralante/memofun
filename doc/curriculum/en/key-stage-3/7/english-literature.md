# Índice
- Drama — A Shakespeare play studied in full: Macbeth, Romeo and Juliet, A Midsummer Night's Dream, or The Tempest
- Drama — The play in performance: how the stage directions shape meaning
- Drama — A Shakespeare sonnet: saying it aloud and noticing the rhyme
- Drama — A modern play: The Curious Incident of the Dog in the Night-Time (Mark Haddon, adapted by Simon Stephens)
- Novel — A Victorian novel: Oliver Twist (Dickens), Jane Eyre (Brontë), or The Hound of the Baskervilles (Conan Doyle)
- Novel — A 20th-century novel: The Boy in the Striped Pyjamas (Boyne), Private Peaceful (Morpurgo), or Skellig (Almond)
- Novel — A modern novel: Wonder (Palacio), The Hunger Games (Collins), or Refugee Boy (Zephaniah)
- Novel — Saying who tells the story and why that matters
- Novel — Saying what a writer's choice of language adds to the scene
- Poetry — Poets from the Romantic period: Wordsworth, Coleridge, Keats
- Poetry — Poets from the Victorian period: Tennyson, Browning, Christina Rossetti
- Poetry — Poets of the First World War: Wilfred Owen, Siegfried Sassoon, Rupert Brooke
- Poetry — A poem about identity: who I am, where I belong
- Poetry — A poem about place: the country, the city, the home
- Poetry — A poem about war: reading it with the history in mind
- Poetry — A poem about love: a sonnet by Shakespeare or by a modern poet
- Poetry — Saying how form and structure shape meaning
- Authors — William Shakespeare: the man, the theatre, the era
- Authors — Charles Dickens: the Victorian storyteller
- Authors — Wilfred Owen: the soldier-poet of the Western Front
- Authors — Carol Ann Duffy: the Poet Laureate

# Contexto o notas adicionales
First year of secondary school in England (11-12 years old). Temario
según el English National Curriculum (programmes of study for
English Literature at Key Stage 3) published by the Department for
Education. Year 7 opens KS3 with the first full Shakespeare play, a
proper 19th-century novel, a strong 20th-century and modern novel
block, and the first chronological poetry timeline (Romantics →
Victorians → First World War → modern). Memofun covers the
**literature** strand only — drama, novels, poems, and authors;
phonics, spelling, grammar, punctuation, and handwriting are
intentionally **out of scope** for this subject.
