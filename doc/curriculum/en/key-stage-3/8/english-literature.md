# Índice
- Drama — A second Shakespeare play studied in full: The Merchant of Venice, Much Ado About Nothing, Julius Caesar, or Henry V
- Drama — Shakespeare's villains: Shylock, Macbeth, Iago — and how they persuade us
- Drama — Shakespeare's heroines: Portia, Beatrice, Juliet — and what they choose
- Drama — A modern play: An Inspector Calls (J. B. Priestley) or Blood Brothers (Willy Russell)
- Novel — A second Victorian novel: A Christmas Carol (Dickens), Great Expectations (Dickens), or Pride and Prejudice (Austen)
- Novel — A 20th-century novel: Of Mice and Men (Steinbeck), To Kill a Mockingbird (Lee), or The Boy in the Striped Pyjamas (Boyne)
- Novel — Gothic fiction: Frankenstein (Shelley), Dracula (Stoker), or The Woman in Black (Hill)
- Novel — A modern novel: Noughts & Crosses (Blackman), The Hate U Give (Thomas), or Long Way Down (Reynolds)
- Poetry — Poets of the Second World War and after: Keith Douglas, Ted Hughes, Philip Larkin
- Poetry — Poets writing today: a contemporary poet chosen by the school
- Poetry — Narrative poetry: The Highwayman (Noyes), The Lady of Shalott (Tennyson)
- Poetry — A poem about power: who has it, who wants it
- Poetry — A poem about memory: looking back, holding on
- Authors — Mary Shelley: the mother of science fiction
- Authors — Bram Stoker: Dracula and the Victorian vampire
- Authors — J. B. Priestley: An Inspector Calls and the politics of time
- Authors — Benjamin Zephaniah: poetry that speaks in its own voice
- Authors — Jason Reynolds and Malorie Blackman: contemporary voices

# Contexto o notas adicionales
Second year of secondary school in England (12-13 years old). Temario
según el English National Curriculum (programmes of study for
English Literature at Key Stage 3) published by the Department for
Education. Year 8 widens Year 7 with a second full Shakespeare play,
Gothic fiction (Shelley, Stoker), a wider modern-novel selection
(Steinbeck, Lee, Blackman, Thomas, Reynolds), and a poetry block
that runs from the Second World War to the present day. Memofun
covers the **literature** strand only — drama, novels, poems, and
authors; phonics, spelling, grammar, punctuation, and handwriting are
intentionally **out of scope** for this subject.
